We hypothesize four mechanisms: dependency-driven grounding (leaf-first commits to concrete, evidence-linked propositions), local evidence injection that clusters citations near supported claims, constrained synthesis that reduces cross-section contradictions, and JSON-constrained intermediates that improve auditability.

Expected signatures are improved local/global coherence, tighter citation proximity to claims, and fewer contradictory sentence pairs across sections.

To validate these hypotheses run controlled generation experiments comparing graph-guided leaf-first + per-leaf evidence/JSON against a top-down baseline (with ablations), and evaluate with calibrated metrics: citation coverage, citation proximity/clustering, unsupported-assertion rate, contradiction rate, and human coherence scores.

Control for retrieval quality, model/prompting, and topic heterogeneity; use paired per-document statistical tests with confidence intervals, and supplement quantitative results with trace-level provenance inspection, citation-dispersion plots, and error-typology analyses; conclusions remain provisional until such experiments are performed.
